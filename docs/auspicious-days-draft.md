## Shared intro (shown once, at the top of every day page, collapsed)

**Why some days matter more**

Tibetan Buddhism follows the moon. Teachers explain that the moon affects more than the tides: as it
waxes and wanes it also affects the subtle energies of the body, so on some days the conditions for
practice come together more easily (Lama Döndrup, Sukhasiddhi Foundation). Over the centuries certain
days of each lunar month became linked with particular buddhas and practices.

Practice on these days is said to bring much greater benefit than usual. Teachers give different
figures for how much; what they agree on is the advice: on these days, do a little more, and do it well.

Different schools and centres keep slightly different customs. If your teacher or centre does something
different, follow them.

## 8th · Medicine Buddha Day

**What it is.** A day for the Medicine Buddha (Sangye Menla), the buddha of healing, and for taking a
day of simple vows.

**Why this day.**
- The 8th is one of three days each month (with the full and new moon) traditionally kept with the
  eight one-day vows. Jigme Lingpa's ritual for taking them says: *"From now on, on the eighth and full
  and new moon days, I shall abandon killing…"*
- Jeff Watt (Himalayan Art Resources) lists the 8th as a Medicine Buddha day, citing the
  *Kṣitigarbha Sūtra*. Kagyu centres also keep it for Tārā (Lama Döndrup).

**What people do.**
- *Anyone:* pray for those who are ill, recite the Medicine Buddha mantra
  (TADYATHA OM BEKANDZE BEKANDZE MAHA BEKANDZE RADZA SAMUGATE SOHA), be especially careful not to harm
  any living being today, dedicate the merit to everyone who is suffering.
- *If you'd like to go further:* take the eight one-day vows from sunrise to the next sunrise. In Jigme Lingpa's ritual they begin:
  abandon killing, stealing, sexual misconduct, lying, intoxicants, and singing, dancing and jewellery;
  the rest are listed in the text. **[check the full list against the text before publishing]**
- *If you have the practice:* the Medicine Buddha sādhana (the app links Palpung's text).

**Practise and read more.**
- Medicine Buddha texts: Lotsawa House → `/topics/medicine-buddha/`; Palpung's Medicine Buddha puja (already in Texts).
- The one-day vows: Jigme Lingpa, *The Blissful Path of Action Tantra* (Lotsawa House).

## 10th · Guru Rinpoche Day

**What it is.** The day of Guru Rinpoche (Padmasambhava), who brought Vajrayāna Buddhism to Tibet.
Kept every month, in all Tibetan schools that pray to him **[check: the app currently says "observed
across all Tibetan Buddhist schools"; I'd soften to this]**.

**Why this day.** Gönpo Tseten Rinpoche explains:
- Guru Rinpoche was born miraculously from a lotus on the 10th day of the Monkey month, so the 10th
  of every month recalls his birth and life.
- Before leaving Tibet he promised to return to those with faith, every morning and evening, and on
  every 10th day of the waxing moon. In the words quoted in the text: *"On every tenth day I shall come
  to Tibet."*
- Practice on this day is said to be greatly multiplied: a vast feast offering on the 10th brings
  merit *"a hundred thousand times more exalted than on any other day."*

**What people do.** From the advice Gönpo Tseten Rinpoche quotes:
- *Anyone:* pray to Guru Rinpoche with devotion (the Seven-Line Prayer is the best known). Do whatever
  good you feel drawn to: make offerings, set out water bowls, give to people and animals in need,
  free animals, circumambulate. *"At the least, at sun rise on the tenth day / Do not engage in
  non-virtuous deeds, such as taking lives."*
- *If you have the practice:* Guru Rinpoche sādhanas and a feast offering (tsok).

**Practise and read more.**
- Prayers to Guru Rinpoche: Lotsawa House → `/topics/guru-rinpoche-prayers/` (incl. the Seven-Line Prayer).
- Palpung: Konchog Chidu and Shower of Blessings (already in Texts).
- The whole explanation: Gönpo Tseten Rinpoche, *Explanation of the Tenth Day* (Lotsawa House).

## 15th · Full Moon (Amitābha)

**What it is.** The full moon: a day for Buddha Amitābha, for restoring vows, and for doing everything a
little more mindfully.

**Why this day.**
- The full and new moons are the traditional *sojong* days, the twice-monthly "restoring and purifying"
  of vows observed by monks and nuns, which the Buddha taught "to replenish virtue and purify harmful
  deeds" (verse quoted by Patrul Rinpoche, Rigpa Wiki *Sojong*). Lay people are
  encouraged to keep the eight one-day vows on these days.
- Jeff Watt lists the 15th for Amitābha and for all peaceful deities (citing the *Kṣitigarbha Sūtra*
  for the latter). Lama Döndrup describes it as a day for Amitābha and phowa.
- The Buddha's birth, awakening and passing are remembered on the full moon of the fourth month (Saga
  Dawa Düchen), which is the greatest full moon of the year. **[check: add a source sentence when we add
  festivals, A7]**

**What people do.**
- *Anyone:* pray to Amitābha and make aspiration prayers to be reborn in his pure land (Dewachen);
  recite his name or mantra; look back honestly at the month and make a fresh start; dedicate the merit
  to those who have died.
- *If you'd like to go further:* the eight one-day vows (see the 8th).
- *If you have the practice:* sojong of the three vows, phowa.

**Practise and read more.**
- Amitābha and Dewachen prayers: Lotsawa House → `/topics/amitabha/`, `/topics/sukhavati-aspiration/`.
- Sojong: Lotsawa House → `/topics/sojong/` (e.g. Dudjom Rinpoche's concise sojong of the three vows).

## 25th · Ḍākinī Day

**What it is.** The day of the ḍākinīs: the female embodiments of enlightened wisdom and energy. It is
the 10th day of the *waning* moon, the counterpart of Guru Rinpoche's day on the waxing 10th.

**Why this day.** Gönpo Tseten Rinpoche quotes the *Cakrasaṃvara* root tantra: *"on the tenth day of the
waxing moon and the tenth day of the waning moon (the twenty-fifth day), yoginīs will come."* He
explains that:
- outwardly, the ḍākinīs gather at sacred places and where people practise (Lochen Dharmaśrī, whom he
  also quotes, says this happens on the 25th);
- inwardly, the subtle channels, winds and essences of the body gather too;
- so outer and inner conditions for practice come together, which is why the day is praised as
  supreme.

**What people do.**
- *Anyone:* make offerings (flowers, light, food), pray, and practise with devotion. Many people make a
  simple offering of food and drink and share it afterwards.
- *If you have the practice:* a feast offering (tsok) and ḍākinī practices, such as Vajrayoginī or
  Vajravārāhī. Customs differ by centre: patrulrinpoche.net's centre does Yumka Dechen Gyalmo on the 25th;
  Lama Döndrup (Kagyu) names Vajrayoginī.
- **[check]** The app currently says "a day when transmission is especially available". I found no
  source for this, so I've left it out.

**Practise and read more.**
- Feast offering (tsok): Lotsawa House → `/topics/tsok/`.
- Ḍākinī texts: Lotsawa House → `/topics/dakini-treasury/`.
- The source: Gönpo Tseten Rinpoche, *Explanation of the Tenth Day* (covers both the 10th and 25th).

## 29th · Dharmapāla (Protector) Day

**What it is.** The day before the new moon, kept for the Dharma protectors: enlightened beings, often
wrathful in form, who protect the teachings and those who practise them.

**Why this day.**
- Jeff Watt lists the 9th, 19th and 29th for all the Dharma protectors, Mahākāla and others; the 29th is
  the one most widely kept. Lama Döndrup (Kagyu) gives the 29th to the protectors, and adds cleaning
  the shrine.
- At the end of the year the same day becomes **Gutor** (29th of the 12th month, listed by tibetanbuddhistcalendar.org),
  held just before Losar. **[check: source a sentence on what Gutor is for]**
- **[check]** I have not found a traditional explanation of *why* the 29th specifically. Rather than
  invent one, the page can say so: "It is the custom across Tibetan schools to…"

**What people do.**
- *Anyone:* clean and refresh your shrine; make a smoke offering (sang); ask for obstacles to your
  practice, and to others' wellbeing, to be cleared.
- *If you have the practice:* the protector practices of your lineage (in the Karma Kagyu, Mahākāla
  Bernakchen) with torma offerings.

**Practise and read more.**
- Smoke offering: Mipham's brief sang and Riwo Sangchö (already in Texts); Lotsawa House → `/topics/sang-offering/`.
- Protector texts: Lotsawa House → `/topics/dharma-protectors/`.

## 30th · New Moon

**What it is.** The last day of the lunar month: a day for purification, confession and a fresh start,
and a day of the Buddha Śākyamuni.

**Why this day.**
- Like the full moon, the new moon is a *sojong* day for restoring vows, and one of the three monthly
  days for the eight one-day vows (Jigme Lingpa's ritual).
- Jeff Watt lists the 30th for Śākyamuni Buddha, citing the *Kṣitigarbha Sūtra*. Lama Döndrup
  describes both the full and new moons as days of strong influence on the subtle body, good for
  acknowledging harmful actions.

**What people do.**
- *Anyone:* look back on the month; acknowledge what went wrong without beating yourself up; resolve to
  do better; recite the Vajrasattva mantra or the Thirty-Five Buddhas confession; make offerings to the
  Buddha.
- *If you'd like to go further:* the eight one-day vows.
- *If you have the practice:* sojong of the three vows; Vajrasattva practice (the app already counts
  Vajrasattva / Diamond Mind).

**Practise and read more.**
- Confession and Vajrasattva: Lotsawa House → `/topics/confession/`, `/topics/vajrasattva/`.
- Sojong: Lotsawa House → `/topics/sojong/`.
