Proposal for V10 · 30 Sep 2026

Dharma Practice, rebuilt around the practitioner

A working prototype of the new structure, with the thinking behind it. Tap through it like the real app: count a session, open a prayer, check the calendar, try the breathing guide. The Tibetan date, moon and special days are calculated live with the app's verified calendar. Practice numbers are examples.

Everything inside the phone works. On a real phone the session screen stays awake and gives a small buzz on each tap.

Who opens this app

The committed practitionerDoing ngöndro, often over years. Mostly 40 to 75. Wants to log today's practice fast and never lose the total.
The newcomerJust received the teaching. Unsure what the practices are or what "Dorje Sempa" means. Needs a gentle way in.
The person the pecha shuts outDyslexia, low vision, or simply not "a tech person". Every extra word, tap or small grey label is a barrier.

Where they are: a shrine room, low light, early morning or late evening. The phone is on the cushion or in the lap, often used one-handed while the other hand holds a mala.

What they come to do, most often first

  • Every dayAdd today's practice to their total. Today: 3 taps deep, inside an accordion.
  • Every dayCount while practising. Today: a separate "Tap counter" tab that doesn't know which practice it is for.
  • Most daysSee what day it is. Is it a special day? Today: below three menu tiles on Home.
  • In a sessionRead a prayer while chanting. Today: one long scroll with small type.
  • Now and thenLearn about the refuge tree or a practice.
  • RarelyChange the look, back up, read credits. Today: a whole nav item, and 9 themes on show.

The current app gives each of these the same weight. The redesign puts the top two behind one tap from opening the app.

Six principles

  1. Open to Today. One calm screen answers "what day is it, and what's my practice?" with one big button to begin.
  2. One job per screen. Each screen has one obvious next step. No accordions, no sub-tabs, no hidden menus.
  3. Big and plain. 17px text or larger, buttons at least 48px tall, a word under every icon, no small-caps labels, and no italics for reading. This follows the British Dyslexia Association guide and research on apps for older adults.
  4. Practice, not productivity. A session opens like a practice and closes with dedication of merit. Progress is shown gently ("4 days this week"). There are no streaks to break and no guilt.
  5. Made for the shrine room. The screen stays awake while counting. The whole screen is the tap area. A small buzz marks each tap and a longer one each full mala. Dark mode is properly dim.
  6. Your numbers are safe. Years of accumulations live on one phone. Backing up takes one tap and makes a file you can keep anywhere.

The new structure

Now: 5 places, plus sub-tabsHome · Practise (Accumulate / Tap counter) · Prayers (Prayers / Practice Texts) · Learn · More (Appearance / About)
V10: 4 places, nothing nestedToday: date, special day, your practice, this week
Practice: count, your accumulations, guided meditations
Texts: prayers in a verse-by-verse chant view, practice texts
Learn: refuge tree, the practices explained, calendar, about

Settings move behind the gear on Today. Light, Dark and Automatic sit up front. Your nine themes stay, one tap further in, under "More colour themes".

What makes it feel like home

  • A session that knows what it's for. Start from your current practice and count on a mala ring of 108 beads. Finish, and the count goes into that practice's total. The separate tap counter and "log to practice" step disappear.
  • Dedication at the end. Every session ends with the dedication verse, as it does in the shrine room. Continuing is one tap.
  • The day, in Tibetan terms. "Tibetan day 19 ༡༩ · 8th month · Fire Horse year", with the moon as it really is tonight.
  • A month at a glance. Tap the date to see the whole Tibetan month, with special days marked, including skipped and doubled days.
  • Prayers you can chant from. One verse at a time in large type, with a big Next button at the bottom for your thumb. Tibetan script can be turned on or off.
  • Fonts chosen for reading. Atkinson Hyperlegible, designed by the Braille Institute for low-vision readers, for everything you read. A warm serif (Fraunces) only for headings. Noto Serif Tibetan so the script renders properly on every phone.

Features, in order

V10: the rebuild

  • Today screen, session mode with mala counting, buzz and keep-awake, then log and dedicate
  • Quick add ("+108") from Today, with Undo
  • Tibetan month calendar
  • Verse-by-verse chant view
  • Backup and restore (a file you keep), with clear warnings before replacing anything
  • Add your own practices and counters
  • Your existing counts carry over untouched

V10.1: guided practice

  • Nine-Round Breathing, guided step by step (try it in the prototype), using your exact text
  • White A, drafted from Namkhai Norbu and James Low sources, for your sign-off
  • A short "what is this practice?" explainer on every practice
  • "Add special days to my phone's calendar": one file that gives reminders without accounts or servers

Later, if wanted

  • Recordings of the prayers to chant along with
  • A history of days practised
  • Sangha totals (needs a server and accounts, so it's a bigger step)

Three decisions for you

  1. Fonts: switch reading text to Atkinson Hyperlegible, with a serif only for headings?
  2. First open: ask "What do you practise?" (Karma Kagyu ngöndro, something else, just looking), so Today shows the right practice? Or assume ngöndro?
  3. Look: is this "shrine room" direction right (saffron on rice paper, lamp-lit dark), or do you want a second direction to compare?
